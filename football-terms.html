Football terms explained

Short Football Talk explanations for supporters. These are general summaries; competition-specific rules and the referee’s application of the Laws of the Game matter.

Offside

In general, a player is in an offside position when they are in the opponents’ half and nearer the goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent when a team-mate plays the ball. Position alone is not an offence: involvement in active play matters. There is no offside offence directly from a goal kick, throw-in or corner.

Goal Difference

Goal difference is goals scored minus goals conceded. It can separate teams level on points; the full tie-breaking order depends on the competition.

Stoppage Time

Added time accounts for time lost during a half, such as substitutions, injuries and other delays. The board shows a minimum: further delays can mean the referee plays longer.

Var

VAR means Video Assistant Referee. It assists with certain major match-changing incidents, including goals, penalty decisions, direct red cards and mistaken identity. It does not review every decision.

Points

In the usual league format, a win earns three points, a draw one point and a defeat none. Competition rules can vary.

Extra Time

Extra time is normally two 15-minute periods in a knockout match when the competition requires it. It is different from added time at the end of each half. Some competitions go straight to penalties.

Predicted Lineups

Predicted lineups are forecasts, not confirmed team sheets. Football Talk switches the label when the match data provider supplies confirmed teams. The lineup views refresh while visible; release timing depends on the competition and provider.
