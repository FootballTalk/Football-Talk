Premier League table rules: what happens when teams are level?

Points come first, but they are not the only way clubs can be separated. Here is how the Premier League decides places during the season and at the final table.

By Mark Dauncey, Football Talk · Published 4 October 2026 · 4 minute read

A glance at the Premier League table can lead to a familiar question: two clubs have the same points, so why is one above the other? The short answer is goal difference. The full answer depends on whether you are looking at the standings during the season or a final position that affects the title, relegation or qualification for another competition.

The first three columns that matter

In the Premier League, a win earns three points, a draw earns one and a defeat earns none. Clubs are ordered first by points. If clubs are level, goal difference is used: goals scored minus goals conceded. If that is also level, the club with more goals scored is placed higher.

Quick example
If two teams have 20 points, and one has scored 25 and conceded 15, its goal difference is +10. A team with 22 scored and 18 conceded has +4, so the first team is ahead on goal difference. If both have the same goal difference, goals scored is the next comparison.

These three measures — points, goal difference and goals scored — are the main figures supporters see in the live table. They make the standings easy to follow as matches finish, while remaining consistent throughout the season.

Why a live table can show two clubs level

When clubs are equal on all three figures during the season, the live table may show them as level or use a display order to place one row first. That display order should not be mistaken for a final sporting tiebreak. The Premier League’s end-of-season rules provide additional steps when a tie has to decide a consequential final position.

What happens if the final table is still tied?

If the title, relegation places or qualification for another competition cannot be decided because clubs finish level on points, goal difference and goals scored, the Premier League applies its additional end-of-season criteria. It first compares points gained in the head-to-head league matches between the tied clubs. If that still does not separate them, it compares away goals scored in those head-to-head matches. A play-off can be used if the position still cannot be decided.

That is why a head-to-head result is not the first tiebreaker shown in the ordinary table. It only comes into the final decision after points, goal difference and goals scored are equal, and where the tie concerns a position that must be decided under the competition rules.

If those are level late in the season, head-to-head points and away goals may become relevant under the final table rules.
